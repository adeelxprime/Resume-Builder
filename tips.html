Practical Career Advice

The Complete CV Writing Guide

Recruiters spend an average of 6 to 7 seconds on initial resume scans. Learn how to craft bullet points that prove your impact and pass both automated software and hiring managers.

1. Writing Impactful Bullet Points: Google's "XYZ Formula"

The single biggest mistake candidates make is writing job descriptions instead of accomplishments. Recruiters already know what a software engineer or marketing coordinator is supposed to do—they want to know how well you did it.

Google’s hiring team famously recommends structuring every achievement with the XYZ Formula:

“Accomplished [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z].”

Real-World Before & After Examples:

✕ WEAK (Passive Task Description):
"Responsible for managing social media accounts and growing followers."
✓ STRONG (XYZ Formula with Metrics):
"Grew brand social engagement by 140% across LinkedIn and Twitter within 6 months by developing an educational short-form video series."

2. Writing an Effective Professional Summary

Your professional summary should not be a vague declaration of hopes (e.g., "Motivated worker seeking a challenging position"). Instead, think of it as an executive elevator pitch summarizing:

  • Who you are: Your professional title and years of relevant tenure.
  • What you specialize in: 2 or 3 core competencies or domain specialties.
  • A signature metric: A standout outcome or quantifiable business milestone.
Recommended 3-Sentence Structure:

"Senior Backend Engineer with 7+ years of experience architecting microservices and distributed database systems. Proven track record reducing infrastructure expenses by 35% while maintaining 99.99% system availability. Skilled in Go, Kubernetes, AWS, and event-driven architectures."

3. Curating Skills: Hard vs. Soft Skills

Hiring systems parse resumes for hard technical keywords matched against the job description. Generic soft skills like "good communicator", "hard worker", or "team player" consume valuable space without offering evidence.

Rule of thumb: Demonstrate soft skills through your work history bullets, and reserve the Skills section for verifiable proficiencies:

  • Programming & Tools: Python, TypeScript, React, Docker, SQL, Figma, Git.
  • Methodologies: Agile/Scrum, CI/CD, Test-Driven Development, Root Cause Analysis.
  • Languages: English (Native), Spanish (Fluent), German (Conversational).

4. Formatting for Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)

Many mid-to-large companies utilize Applicant Tracking Systems to catalog candidates before human review. To guarantee your CV parses without errors:

  1. Use Standard Headings: Label sections with conventional names: Work Experience, Education, Skills, and Projects. Avoid clever headings like "My Journey" or "What I Love".
  2. Avoid Image-Only Resumes: Never submit a flattened image (JPEG or PNG) as a resume. An ATS requires real text layers, which CVCraft produces natively.
  3. Keep Formatting Clean: Do not place essential career history inside non-standard vector shapes or intricate multi-nested tables.
  4. Choose PDF with Selectable Text: CVCraft’s PDF export preserves clean text layers for automated parsers.

5. Common CV Pitfalls to Avoid

Avoid these frequent errors:
  • Personal details that shouldn't be on a resume: In the US, UK, and many regions, avoid photos, age, marital status, or full street addresses. City and country/state are sufficient.
  • Generic email addresses: Use a clean address such as first.last@gmail.com rather than old nicknames.
  • Unexplained employment gaps: Brief, honest framing (e.g., "Career sabbatical for professional certification and family care") is widely accepted.
  • Typos and inconsistent dates: Inconsistencies between LinkedIn and your resume damage credibility immediately.
